Know Your Rights

The Constitution guarantees your rights. The Municipal Systems Act empowers you. When Emfuleni fails to deliver, you have legal recourse — and ActionSA Ward 15 will help you use it.

Right to Basic Services

Section 27 of the Constitution guarantees access to sufficient water. Section 24 guarantees a healthy environment. When Emfuleni cuts water or allows sewage to flow in streets, they violate your constitutional rights. The class action we joined is built on this legal foundation.

Municipal Bylaws

Emfuleni has bylaws covering noise control, public nuisances, building regulations, and waste management. These laws exist to protect you. ActionSA Ward 15 is documenting where the municipality fails to enforce its own laws — and we will use that evidence to hold them accountable.

What To Do When Govt Fails

2. Report to the Municipal Manager in writing.
3. Escalate to the Public Protector for maladministration.

June 3, 2026

Emfuleni Is Ripe for Change: The Tide Turns in the Vaal

The collapse of governance in Emfuleni is no longer a story being told from the outside. It is something residents live through every month — on their billing statements, at their taps, and at the end of a phone line that nobody answers. The municipality has not merely underperformed. It has systematically failed the people it was built to serve, and the community has quietly reached the end of its patience.

The numbers confirm what residents already know. An independent oversight audit uncovered 32 distinct administrative and infrastructure faults across the municipality — not isolated incidents, but evidence of a structural breakdown that runs through every department. These are not problems that can be smoothed over with a press release or a new committee. They are the fingerprints of an institution that has stopped functioning.

The community's response has been proportionate. A water billing class action is being prepared for the High Court, targeting the Mayor, the Municipal Manager, and the Speaker directly. The legal argument is straightforward: residents have been billed for services that were never delivered, using estimates that bear no relationship to actual meter readings. That is not administrative error. It is financial exploitation, and it has gone on long enough to attract serious legal attention.

The political picture is shifting too. The DA's recent capture of Ward 28 in Beverly Hills was not a fluke. It was a signal — readable to anyone paying attention — that voters in this region are done waiting for the ruling party to fix itself from within. The old loyalties are loosening. People are making new calculations.

Into that opening, a disciplined ground operation in Ward 15 is quietly building something durable. The focus is not on noise. It is on structure: a centralized data platform that registers affected ratepayers, documents billing irregularities, and converts individual grievances into collective legal and electoral weight. Every resident who uploads a statement becomes part of something larger than a complaint.

The argument to regional leadership is not complicated. The legal framework is in place. The ground structures are growing. The community is ready. Emfuleni has not been ungovernable — it has simply been badly governed. That is a problem with a solution, and the window to deliver it is open right now.
